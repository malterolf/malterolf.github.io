Aortic dissection is a life-threatening disease in which a tear in the inner layer of the aortic wall allows blood to separate the wall layers. The interdisciplinary LEAD project of Graz University of Technology brought together mechanics, imaging, numerical methods and clinical expertise to understand how this disease initiates and progresses.

My doctoral research within the project focused on material and computational modeling of the diseased aortic wall. We developed models for degraded elastic fibers and for the orientation and function of smooth muscle cells, quantified the effect of stochastic inhomogeneities in the aortic wall with Bayesian surrogate models, and studied aortic remodeling in chronic type B dissection with patient-specific fluid–structure interaction simulations in collaboration with Stanford University.

I also organized the LEAD project workshops on mechanics, modeling and simulation of aortic dissection in 2020 and 2024.
